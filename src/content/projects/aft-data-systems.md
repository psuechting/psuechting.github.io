## The problem

Before this work, locals in the federation tracked their members and organizing in ad hoc spreadsheets, alongside a national membership database that has no features usable at the local level. Everything was either entirely manual or one-size-fits-the-entire-national-federation. Nothing was built around how a local actually organizes.

## What I built

A relational Airtable database for each local, specified to that local's context, holding the pieces organizing runs on: membership and employment records, a contact-information update system, an event system, and workplace organizing tracking. Around the local bases sits a federation-level ecosystem that moves data in and out without re-keying:

<ol class="flow">
	<li><strong>Intake</strong><span>Administrative data arrives in shared drive folders; scripts prepare import files and a sync service maps each file to the local's schema and loads the records.</span></li>
	<li><strong>Local base</strong><span>Each local's relational database, where staff and leaders track members, events and organizing conversations.</span></li>
	<li><strong>Forms</strong><span>Registration and custom forms feed the bases directly, so sign-ups and member-interest data land where organizers work.</span></li>
	<li><strong>Outreach</strong><span>Lists flow out to email and texting tools and to a phonebanking system that pushes call lists out and brings call outcomes back.</span></li>
</ol>

At the federation level, a base that tracks locals, leaders, contracts and contract campaigns also keeps the federation's leader listservs in sync automatically. In 2026 I began mapping the entire ecosystem, every platform, system and data flow, as a single shared picture, so the federation can plan from one source of truth.

## Scale

- **8 locals** run on databases I built, with **3 more** in progress, out of 29 in the federation
- **10 federation staff** work in the systems

## Outcome

- Supported local **membership drives**
- Supported the **first community college faculty and classified staff strike in Oregon's history**
- Now the backbone for integrating the federation's whole data ecosystem

## How the role grew

This began as contract work building databases for local labor federations. What won the federation over was that the databases actually served local membership organizing and engagement, where earlier systems were manual or designed for the national federation as a whole. The contract work grew into a statewide contract with AFT Oregon and then, in 2025, a full-time appointment as the federation's Data Specialist.
